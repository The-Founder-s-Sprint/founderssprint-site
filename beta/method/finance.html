Most businesses here don't fail because the idea was wrong. They fail because they ran out of money before the idea had time to work — and never saw it coming. In a market where a bank loan is rare and venture capital is rarer, the founders who survive are the ones who can hold their own numbers in their head: what a customer costs, what they're worth, how long the cash lasts, and what the company is actually worth to an investor. This page breaks down the ten specialties of that craft, and why each one earns its place in a serious founder's toolkit.

Sub-category 01 · Unit Economics

If a single sale doesn't make sense, a million of them won't either.

The economics of one customer — understood before you scale the losses.

80%
of African startups fail within their first five years — most because the economics never added up.African Development Bank
CAC & LTV

What it costs to win a customer, against what that customer is worth over their lifetime. Get this ratio wrong and growth just burns cash faster. We help you measure both honestly — and find the gap where profit actually lives.

Payback Period

How many months until a customer pays back what you spent to acquire them. In a cash-tight market, a long payback can sink you even when the unit looks "profitable" on paper. We pin down how fast your money comes home.

Contribution Margin

What's left from each sale after the costs that scale with it — the real fuel for everything else. Founders who know their contribution margin know exactly how much room they have to grow, discount, or survive a slow month.

Sub-category 02 · Financial Planning

A plan is just a guess until the cash flow proves it.

Forecasting, runway, and the discipline of not running out.

9%
of Uganda's MSMEs are formally registered — the rest run with no books to plan from.UNCTAD, 2023
Revenue Forecasting

A forecast you can defend — built from real drivers, not a wishful straight line. It's the backbone of every budget, hire, and pitch. We teach you to model revenue you can actually stand behind in front of an investor.

Burn Rate & Runway

How fast you're spending, and how many months that leaves you. The single number that decides how much time you have to make it work. Knowing your runway to the week is the difference between a calm decision and a panicked one.

Cash Flow Management

Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact. Plenty of growing businesses die solvent on paper but dry in the bank. We build the cash-flow view that tells you what you can spend this week — and what you can't.

Tax & Compliance

URA, VAT, and the obligations that quietly compound into a crisis when ignored. Building tax into the model from the start keeps a growing business from being ambushed by its own success. We make compliance a line in the plan, not a year-end shock.

Sub-category 03 · Capital Architecture

Raising money is the easy part. Structuring it so you keep your company is the craft.

Valuation, ownership, and the terms you'll live with for years.

<10%
of Ugandan family businesses survive beyond the founding generation — ownership is rarely built to last.Economic Policy Research Centre
Valuation Methods

What your company is worth, and why — in language an investor respects. A number you can justify turns a negotiation from hope into evidence. We ground your valuation in method, not in what you wish it were.

Cap Table Design

Who owns what, and what's left for the people who haven't joined yet. A messy cap table can quietly kill a deal years later. We help you design ownership that still makes sense three rounds from now.

Term Sheet Analysis

The clauses that decide control, dilution, and who really runs the company. Founders sign terms they don't fully understand and pay for it for years. We teach you to read a term sheet like the people on the other side of the table.
