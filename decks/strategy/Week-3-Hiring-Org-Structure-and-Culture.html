Strategy & Team · Week 3Sequence hires to milestones
Timing is the whole game

Hire too early and you burn cash; too late and you're the bottleneck.

Match to milestones

Each hire should be funded by a revenue or traction milestone you've actually hit.

Hire your weakness

The first hires should cover what you're worst at — not clone what you're already good at.

One owner per gap

Hire when a real outcome has no clear owner — not because everyone "feels busy".

The discipline: name the constraint, hire the person who removes it, and make sure the milestone pays for them.

Strategy & Team · Week 3An objective vetting frame
Chemistry is not enough

Test the relationship before the stakes are irreversible.

A co-founder choice is the highest-leverage, hardest-to-reverse decision you'll make. Liking each other is the easy part. Vet deliberately for shared vision, complementary skills, real commitment, and how you handle conflict — then protect it with vesting and a written agreement.

Before you sign

"Have we worked through one real disagreement — and survived it well?"

Strategy & Team · Week 3Three values, written as behaviour
A value you can't observe is decoration

Pick three values — and define the behaviour that proves each.

Exactly three

More than three and none of them are non-negotiable. Three you'll actually defend.

Tied to behaviour

For each value, name what it looks like in action — and what violates it.

Hired and fired by

A real value is one you'll hire for, promote for, and let someone go for.

The founder models it first. Culture is simply the behaviour you repeatedly tolerate and reward.

Strategy & Team · Week 3Org structure · accountability
Org design in one rule

Every important outcome has exactly one owner.

One owner

If two people own it, no one does. Map every key outcome to a single name.

Clear lines

Everyone can say who decides what. Ambiguity is where execution quietly dies.

Protect it on paper

Roles, equity and decision rights — written down and vested, before they're tested.

Our own founding team did this early: five founders, defined discipline roles, 4-year vesting with a 1-year cliff, and a clear majority rule for decisions. Structure first, so trust survives growth.
