Strategy & Team · Week 2Anchoring market perception
A decision, not a tagline

Position is the place you own when the customer thinks of the problem.

It's a choice

To stand for one thing clearly, you must give up standing for everything.

It's relative

Positioning only means something against the alternatives the customer already knows.

It's validated

If customers can't repeat it back, it isn't positioning — it's wishful thinking.

The test: a customer who's never met you can explain what you are and who you're for in one breath.

Strategy & Team · Week 2Moats that compound
A feature can be copied by Friday — a moat cannot

Build advantages that compound, not features that get cloned.

A well-capitalised rival can copy any single feature you ship. What they can't easily copy is a structural advantage that took you years and customers to build — and that gets stronger every month you operate. That's a moat. Most early companies have none yet; the job is to start digging one deliberately.

The moat test

"Does this advantage get harder to copy as we grow — or easier?"

Strategy & Team · Week 2Self-guided worksheet · in session
Self-guided worksheet · do it now

Make yourself defensible in 4 moves.

⏱ 25 min solo · 35 min group
1
Your statement
Position it
Write your full positioning sentence — all five parts.
Example"For Kampala pharmacies who run out of stock, our credit-restock app keeps shelves full."
2
Your moat
Name the one
Which single moat is most real for you — and how will you deepen it?
Example"Switching costs — once we hold their order history, leaving is painful."
3
Your signals
What to watch
One signal each for bull and bear conditions.
Example"Bear: the shilling drops 15%. Bull: repeat orders pass 60%."
4
Your moves
Pre-decide
The move you'll make in each case — written now, not later.
Example"Bear: cut to two divisions. Bull: hire a second sales rep."

How we'll review: each founder reads their positioning sentence aloud — and names the one moat they're betting on.
