Product & Pricing · Week 4Strategy before model
Don't pick a model — answer the questions, and it picks itself

Strategy is the “why”; the model is the “how”.

A pricing strategy is the intent — win share, maximise margin, signal premium. A pricing model is the mechanism that delivers it — subscription, freemium, tiered, and so on. Get the strategy right and the model follows. Pick a model first and you'll force the wrong strategy onto your business.

The four questions
  • Who is the customer?
  • What value do they receive?
  • How much will they pay?
  • How does competition shape it?

Product & Pricing · Week 4The two ends of the menu
Most founders default to the worst model

Cost-plus is easy. Value-based is where the money is.

Cost-plus

Cost + markup. Easy, predictable, common — and it completely ignores what the customer values or the market will bear.

Cost 100 + 30% = price 130

Value-based

Price to the perceived value the customer receives. Higher margins, stronger differentiation, and the model investors love — but it takes research.

Worth 500 to them → price 350

Between these two ends sit a dozen models — each a different answer to “how do we charge?” Tap through them next.

Product & Pricing · Week 4How real brands charge
The same menu, chosen for different markets

Five brands, five deliberate model choices.

MTN

Tiered & bundle pricing — data, voice and SMS bundles that raise order value and segment by budget.

Netflix

Subscription tiers — recurring revenue, predictable, with Basic/Standard/Premium choice.

Spotify & Canva

Freemium — free hooks the user; premium converts the committed. Tiers lock them in.

SafeBoda

Dynamic & usage-based — price flexes with demand, distance and time; you pay per ride.

Apple

Premium & skimming — high price signals exclusivity, then eases as new models launch.

The lesson

None copied a rival. Each chose the model that fit their value and market.
