Product & Pricing · Week 2Two kinds of cost
Sort every cost into one of two buckets

Fixed costs stay; variable costs follow every sale.

Fixed costs

The same whether you sell one unit or a thousand.

  • Rent
  • Salaries
  • Software licences
  • Utilities

Variable costs

Rise and fall with each unit you produce or sell.

  • Raw materials
  • Commissions
  • Transaction fees
  • Delivery

Watch “variable creep”: the small per-sale costs — card fees, packaging, last-mile delivery — that silently eat the margin you thought you had.

Product & Pricing · Week 2Cost–volume–profit, in three lines
From one sale to profit, step by step

Three simple equations run your whole P&L.

Contribution / unit

Selling price − variable cost

What each sale leaves to cover fixed costs.

Break-even units

Fixed costs ÷ contribution

Units needed before profit begins.

Profit

Contribution × volume − fixed

Everything above break-even is yours.

Raising price lifts contribution per unit — which lowers your break-even and pulls profit forward. The two weeks connect.

Product & Pricing · Week 2Gross margin & the product mix
Two more numbers that decide where to focus

Lead with the products that carry the margin.

Gross margin

Revenue minus the direct cost of goods sold (COGS). The headline profitability number — and the first thing investors check.

Product mix

Which products are most profitable to make and sell. Push the high-contribution ones; rethink the rest.

The 80/20

Often a few products carry most of the margin. Find them, price them well, and build around them.

Revenue tells you how big you are. Margin tells you whether you'll survive — and which products deserve your attention.

Product & Pricing · Week 2Self-guided worksheet · in session
Self-guided worksheet · do it now

Compute your floor in 4 moves.

⏱ 25 min solo · 35 min group
1
Your variable cost
Per unit
Add up every cost that moves with each sale — including the hidden ones.
Example"Materials + fees + delivery = UGX 35,000."
2
Your fixed costs
Per month
Rent, salaries, software, utilities — the costs that don't move.
Example"Roughly UGX 6,000,000 a month."
3
Your contribution
Per unit
Selling price minus variable cost.
Example"75,000 − 35,000 = UGX 40,000."
4
Your break-even
Units / month
Fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit.
Example"6,000,000 ÷ 40,000 = 150 units."

How we'll review: each founder states their break-even in units — and whether last month was above or below it.
