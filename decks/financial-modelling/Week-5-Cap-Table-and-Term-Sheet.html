Four things a cap table has to show — if you build it forward

What a cap table actually tracks.

Fully-diluted ownership

Every stake including the option pool and convertibles as if already converted — not just issued shares.

The option pool

Shares set aside for future hires. Where it's carved from decides who pays for it.

Convertibles

SAFEs and notes that become equity at the next priced round — model them as converted, not as debt.

Forward-modelled

Ownership through the next one or two rounds — not a snapshot of today.

Not legal advice

This session builds the ownership structure into your model. A lawyer drafts and confirms the instruments before you sign.

Financial Modelling · Week 5The same 10% pool, carved from two different places
Same pool, two very different owners of the cost

Who bears the cost of the option pool?

Pool in pre-money

The more common investor ask. Carved out before the investment, so it dilutes existing shareholders — mostly the founders. The investor's percentage is protected, and it effectively lowers the real pre-money you're accepting.

Pool in post-money

Carved out after the round, so the dilution is shared across everyone — founders and the new investor alike. Rarer, and materially better for the founder.

A pre-money pool increase is a founder-only dilution dressed up as routine housekeeping. Model it explicitly — and know it's negotiable — before you agree to the number.

Financial Modelling · Week 5Market standard versus a term worth pausing on
Know the standard before you negotiate the exception

Standard is a starting point, not a surrender.

ClauseMarket standardWatch for
Liquidation pref.1× non-participatingParticipating, or >1× multiple
Anti-dilutionBroad-based weighted averageFull ratchet
Board seatsProportional to ownershipBroad vetoes / lost control
Founder vesting4-year, standardAggressive leaver terms
Option poolRight-sized, sharedOversized, pre-money

A clause that's “market standard” in San Francisco may not be in Kampala — and the reverse. Treat standard as the opening position, then negotiate the two or three that actually move control.

Financial Modelling · Week 5Worked example · a 1× non-participating preference
Investor put UGX 1B at a UGX 5B post-money (20%) · 1× non-participating

The same clause, two very different exits.

Exit valueInvestor takesFounders / common take
UGX 800M (below preference)UGX 800M (all of it)UGX 0
UGX 10B (well above)UGX 2B (converts to 20%)UGX 8B

1× non-participating is the founder-friendly case — on a big exit the investor takes the greater of their money back OR their ownership, not both. Participating preferred would take the 1B first and 20% of the rest; on a small exit, the preference still comes out ahead of common.

Financial Modelling · Week 5Founders get diligenced — founders should diligence back
A term sheet is the start of a long relationship, not just a transaction

Due diligence runs both directions.

On the investor

Their track record, sector experience, and how actively they've been involved in past portfolio companies. Call founders they've backed — especially ones whose rounds didn't go to plan.

On the terms

Whether each clause matches what's standard for your stage and market. A strong headline valuation with a punitive liquidation preference or lost board control trades a number for lasting leverage.

Not legal advice

This session gets you to a marked-up term sheet — each clause flagged standard, negotiate, or walk-away. A lawyer does the formal review before you sign.
