Modelling here isn't retrospective accounting — it's predictive discipline. Before a shilling is spent, the model states what it's for; after, it's checked against what happened.
The Founder's SprintBuild with direction.
Financial Modelling · Week 1Know what every shilling is supposed to do — and prove it
Profit is an opinion; cash is a fact

Built around the fact, not the opinion.

Before a shilling is spent, the model states what that shilling is supposed to do. After it's spent, the model gets checked against what actually happened. This discipline is built around cash — the fact — not profit, the opinion.

Why it matters

Investors don't fund the best idea in the room. They fund the founder whose numbers they believe.

Financial Modelling · Week 1Self-guided worksheet · in session
Orient your model · do it now

Turn context into assumptions in 4 moves.

⏱ 20 min solo · plain numbers
1
Where you want to be
12-month target
One revenue or growth number your model forecasts toward.
Example"UGX 300m ARR by this time next year."
2
Your revenue drivers
2–3 levers
The handful of things that actually move revenue.
Example"Outlets × baskets/week × average basket."
3
Your monthly burn
One number
Roughly what leaves the account each month, gross.
Example"About UGX 18m a month, salaries-heavy."
4
Your biggest cash gap
Name it
Where cash gets stuck between earning it and collecting it.
Example"Schools pay 60 days after invoicing."

Bring these four to your first Revenue Forecasting session — we turn them straight into model assumptions. Plain numbers over polish; a rough honest figure beats a precise guess.
