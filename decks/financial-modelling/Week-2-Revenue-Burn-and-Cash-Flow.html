Financial Planning · Revenue Forecasting
A forecast, not
a hockey stick.
Most founder forecasts start from a target and work backward into a growth rate that fits. We do the opposite — start from what actually drives revenue, and let the forecast fall out of it.
The Founder's SprintBuild with direction.
Financial Modelling · Week 2Build from drivers, not from the number you wish for
Less impressive on slide one · holds up on slide twelve

Start from pipeline, conversion, pricing and retention.

A forecast built backward from “we need UGX 2B by year 3” collapses the moment an investor asks where the growth comes from. One built from named levers — dealers, sell-through, price — is defensible line by line. This is the revenue tab every other tab in the model depends on.

The rule

A single blended growth rate is a placeholder, not a forecast — replace it with drivers as soon as you have three months of real data.

Use a trailing average, never the most recent month

Cash balance, divided by what actually erodes it.

TermFormulaResult

RunwayCash balance / net burn8.6 months
Burn multipleNet burn / net new revenuebelow 1× = efficient

A burn multiple below 1× is capital-efficient; well above 2× is worth a hard look at what the spend is actually buying.

Financial Modelling · Week 2Same cash balance · runway moves with your decisions
The runway number isn't fixed — model the decision before you make it

One balance. Three futures.

Base case

Current trajectory · net burn UGX 29.2M/mo. Runway: 8.2 months. No new hires, no new spend.

Hiring plan

Two hires in month 2 · net burn rises to ~UGX 38M/mo. Runway: ~6.3 months. Model the hire before the offer.

Cost discipline

A 10% opex cut · net burn falls to ~UGX 25M/mo. Runway: ~9.6 months. Roughly six extra weeks.

Where it breaks: a single-month snapshot treated as normal, lumpy annual costs (insurance, licences) ignored until they hit, and rounding 8.2 up to “9–10” — investors do the arithmetic themselves.

Financial Planning · Cash Flow Management
Profit is an opinion.
Cash is a fact.
A business can show a profit and still miss payroll, because the cash behind that profit is sitting in an unpaid invoice or on a shelf. This is about finding where cash gets stuck.
The Founder's SprintBuild with direction.
Financial Modelling · Week 2The number behind most “profitable but broke” businesses
Worked example · an agro-processing business

55 days of funding your own operations.

MetricFormulaDays
Days Inventory Outstanding(Avg inventory / COGS) × 36545
Days Sales Outstanding(Avg receivables / Revenue) × 36530
Days Payable Outstanding(Avg payables / COGS) × 36520
Cash Conversion CycleDIO + DSO − DPO55

For 55 days on every shilling of cost, the business funds itself before a customer pays. That gap is covered by reserves, a credit line, or renegotiated terms — it doesn’t vanish because the P&L shows a profit. Lower is better.
