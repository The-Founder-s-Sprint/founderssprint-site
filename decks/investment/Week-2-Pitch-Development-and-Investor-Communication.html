Investment Readiness · Week 2Pitch deck design & visual communication
A deck is read in minutes, not studied

Make it skimmable, make it honest.

One idea per slide

A headline that states the point; the slide just proves it. If it needs explaining, it's two slides.

Lead with traction

Investors look at it first. Put your strongest number where the eye lands.

Numbers, not adjectives

"40% month-on-month" beats "huge growth." Every claim has a number behind it.

Clean & consistent

One font system, lots of white space, readable charts. Polish signals professionalism.

A confusing slide makes an investor wonder what else is confusing. Clarity in the deck signals clarity in the founder.

An organised folder, ready before they ask

The proof behind every claim.

Company & cap table

Registration documents and a clean record of who owns what.

Financial model + history

Your projections and the actual historical numbers behind them.

Key contracts

Customer, partner and supplier agreements that de-risk the story.

Product & traction metrics

Usage, retention and revenue data — the evidence, not the claim.

Founder details

Bios, IDs and references — the people the investor is backing.

The deck + one-pager

The story itself, plus a one-page summary they can forward internally.

Investment Readiness · Week 2Most rounds are lost in the silence
Fundraising is a relationship, not a transaction

Investors back founders they've watched execute.

Warm intros open doors

Investors back people introduced by people they trust. Map who can introduce you — cold rarely converts.

Follow up fast

Within 24 hours: thank-you, the answers you promised, the deck, the data-room link, a clear next step.

Keep them updated

Short, regular notes on traction. A 'no, not yet' becomes a 'yes' once they've seen you deliver.

A founder who follows up well stands out more than one with a prettier deck. Most rounds are lost in the silence after the meeting.
