Investors back founders they've watched execute — so let them watch.Joseph Kalema

A clean monthly update — wins, metrics, lowlights, and a specific ask — is the cheapest, highest-return thing a founder can do after a raise. Silence breeds doubt; consistency breeds trust.

The same investor who funds this round usually leads or fills the next. Keep them close and your next raise is half-done before it starts.

Investment Readiness · Week 5Managing multiple investor relationships
Several backers, one focused founder

Keep everyone informed — without losing your week.

One update, many readers

A single monthly note to all investors beats ten one-off calls. Add 1:1 time only where it earns its keep.

Lean on your lead

Your lead investor is your ally — let them help corral and reassure the others.

No surprises

Bad news travels best when it travels early and from you. Surprises destroy trust fastest.

Protect your focus: investor management is a system, not an inbox. Done well, it buys you back time.

Investment Readiness · Week 5Exit · the return event
Plan for it from the start

Every investor needs a way out — eventually.

The return event

Acquisition is the common path in Africa; IPOs are rare. It's how your investors realise their return.

It shapes who you take

An investor's exit timeline must match yours. Patient, catalytic capital expects a longer horizon.

Build optionality

Clean cap table, clean records, real traction — the things that make any exit possible later.

You don't plan the exit to sell tomorrow — you plan it so the choices stay open when the moment comes.

Investment Readiness · Week 5Impact, ESG & reporting expectations
What capital with a development mandate expects

Treat reporting as an asset, not a chore.

Impact measurement

Track and report development outcomes — not just revenue.

ESG standards

Fair labour, safeguarding, environmental care, clean governance — sometimes audited.

Gender & inclusion

Many DFI-backed funds have explicit mandates — an advantage if it's your story.

Governance discipline

Clean records, proper accounts, a functioning board — more than a typical angel deal.

The startups that win DFI-linked capital are the ones who can show their impact as clearly as their revenue.
