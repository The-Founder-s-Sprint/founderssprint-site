Investment Readiness · Week 4Pre-seed instruments
Three ways early equity arrives

SAFEs and notes let you delay the hard valuation.

SAFE

Money now for equity later, at the next priced round. Fast, cheap, founder-friendly.

Convertible note

Like a SAFE, but it's debt — interest and a maturity date, with repayment risk.

Priced equity round

Agree a valuation today and sell shares directly. More legal work; needed for larger raises.

At the earliest stage your company is hardest to value — SAFEs and notes defer that fight to a round with more evidence.

Investment Readiness · Week 4Valuation & dilution — the basics
Two words that decide your ownership

Pre-money, post-money, and the share you sell.

Pre-money

The agreed value of the company before the new money goes in.

Post-money

Pre-money + the amount raised. The value immediately after.

Ownership sold

Money raised ÷ post-money valuation. That's the slice you hand over.

The one formula to remember

Ownership sold = money raised ÷ (pre-money + money raised)

Investment Readiness · Week 4Dilution across rounds
Watch the cumulative effect

Protect ownership round after round.

15–25% per round

The healthy band. Sell too much early and you're demotivated — and unfundable later.

The option pool bites

Shares set aside for future hires dilute founders too — watch where it's created.

Think three rounds ahead

Model pre-seed → seed → Series A together. Each round compounds the last.

Founders who take equity too early give away too much of the company for too little — because early on, your equity is worth the least.

You don't need to be a lawyer. You need to understand the handful of terms that genuinely affect founders — then get a lawyer for the rest.

Investment Readiness · Week 4Founder-friendly negotiation
Negotiate the terms, not just the price

A fair deal you understand beats a high valuation you don't.

Run a parallel process

Competing interest is your strongest leverage. Compare offers on terms, not just headline valuation.

Trade thoughtfully

Concede on what costs you little; hold firm on control, liquidation preference, and the option pool.

Get a lawyer

For the actual documents. This course is educational — a real adviser reviews the binding contract.

The relationship matters too — you'll work with this investor for years. Negotiate hard, but negotiate to a partnership.
