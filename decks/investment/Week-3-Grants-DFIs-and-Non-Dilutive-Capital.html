Investment Readiness · Week 3Grants & their hidden costs
Proof-of-concept capital

The right first money — used with discipline.

Time

Strong applications take days to weeks. Chasing grants that don't fit is a huge hidden drain.

Restrictions

Money is usually earmarked for specific activities — it can't be spent freely.

Reporting

Progress reports, receipts, impact data — a genuine administrative load.

Timing

Fixed cycles and slow disbursement; rarely arrives when cash is most urgent.

Used well, a respected grant or competition win is independent validation — it makes the next investor conversation far easier.

Investment Readiness · Week 3Anatomy of a winning application
Prepare strong answers once, then tailor

Six components decide the outcome.

Sharp problem

Who suffers, how badly, how many — with a number wherever possible.

Clear solution

What you do, for whom, and why it works better than the alternative.

Evidence of traction

Any proof: users, revenue, pilots, partnerships, letters of intent.

Measurable impact

Outcomes the funder cares about — farmers reached, jobs, emissions — with targets.

Credible budget

Exactly how the money is used and what it achieves. Specific beats vague.

The team

Why you're the right people, and who you partner with to deliver.

Investment Readiness · Week 3Stacking · each win de-risks the next
The method
🇺🇬 Grants are not mutually exclusive

Stack the wins to build value.

Win a small grant

A TEF or Hi-Innovator cheque funds the prototype and first proof.

Win a bigger one

Use that proof to win a larger challenge-fund cheque (USD 25k–500k+).

Then raise

Use both as credibility to raise a pre-seed — each win de-risking the next.

Sequenced well, non-dilutive capital builds the company's value before any equity is ever sold.

Investment Readiness · Week 3Development finance institutions
Impact and return — the dual mandate

The largest source of VC on the continent.

A DFI is a government-backed or multilateral investor whose mandate is development outcomes — jobs, inclusion, climate, growth — while still earning a return. That dual mandate explains everything about how they behave.

Without them, much of the local VC industry would not exist. Understanding them isn't optional — it's understanding where the money originates.

The major DFIs in Africa

  • IFC (World Bank Group)
  • British International Investment
  • Proparco (France)
  • FMO (Netherlands), DEG (Germany)
  • AfDB & peers

Investment Readiness · Week 3How an early founder accesses DFI-linked capital
Become attractive to the funds DFIs finance

Speak the impact language.

Target DFI-backed funds

Research which regional funds count DFIs among their investors; approach them like any seed investor.

Join DFI-supported programmes

Accelerators and challenge funds that are development-funded put you inside the pipeline.

Articulate your impact

Jobs, inclusion, climate, gender — the fund must report it upward to its DFI backers.

Build for the long game

Direct DFI investment becomes realistic at Series A — the relationships you build now position you.

Expect ESG and impact reporting that purely commercial money doesn't require. Many DFI-backed funds favour women-led or inclusion-focused ventures — an advantage if that's your story.
