The principles — and the traps.

Write it like this
  • Specific over vague — “UGX 220m ARR, +12%/qtr,” not “strong growth”
  • Lead with your best number — let proof persuade
  • Skimmable — short paragraphs; bold the key line per block
  • One page, ruthlessly edited
  • Active, confident, jargon-free — tailor the ask to the reader
Traps to avoid
  • Walls of dense text no one will skim
  • Hype words — “revolutionary,” “disruptive” — with no proof
  • Vague metrics that hide rather than reveal
  • No clear ask — the reader doesn't know the next step
  • Numbers that don't match your deck or model

Pitch Craft · Session 2The one-pager & the teaser email
Same story, two delivery vehicles

One you attach; one you forward.

The one-pager

  • Structured around the eight blocks
  • Attachable; sent once there's interest
  • Skimmable in ~60 seconds, numbers-led
  • Mirrors your deck — same story, same figures

The teaser email

  • Five lines, forwardable, for cold or warm intros
  • What you do · traction · the raise · why them · the ask
  • One clear ask: a 20-minute call
  • Built to survive being forwarded by a busy contact

Both must mirror the deck and the model. Same hook, same numbers, same ask — inconsistency is the fastest way to lose trust.
