Marketing & Branding · Week 2Preparing to be talked about
Branding 101

Six pieces every brand needs.

Brand identity

What makes you recognisable — name, logo, colour, tone of voice.

Brand promise

What you commit to deliver, every time. It must differentiate.

Brand positioning

How you're perceived versus competitors in the customer's mind.

Brand personality

The human traits you embody — fun, expert, bold, reliable.

Brand story

The facts and feelings of why you exist. The narrative people repeat.

Brand strategy

The long-term plan that keeps all five consistent as you grow.

🇺🇬 SafeBoda · Kampala

One word: safety.

Positioning

How our own mark works

Five petals, one per discipline, converge on a shared centre — a constellation founders navigate by. The idea is the identity: the mark, the colours, and the tagline all say the same thing without explaining it.

  • A combination mark — symbol plus wordmark.
  • A disciplined palette — ink, paper, one accent per surface.
  • Three typefaces — display, label, body. No drift.
  • The tagline does the work — we never explain the metaphor.

Marketing & Branding · Week 2Brand storytelling
Narrative architecture

People don't buy what you do. They buy why.

Why you exist

The conviction behind the company — the change you want to see.

The villain

The problem or status quo your customer is fighting.

The customer is hero

Not you. You're the guide who hands them the tool.

The transformation

Who they become after you. That's the story they repeat.

Tell it consistently everywhere — site, social, packaging, the way you answer the phone. Told once it's a campaign; told everywhere, it becomes a brand.

Marketing & Branding · Week 2Voice & messaging framework
Say it the same way, everywhere

A voice is three or four words you obey.

Pick voice traits

e.g. warm, rigorous, plain-spoken. Three is enough. Write them down.

Define do / don't

"We say founder, never user." Concrete rules beat vague vibes.

One message, repeated

A single core line every channel echoes — until customers can finish it.

The consistency test

Cover the logo on your post, your email, and your packaging. Could a stranger tell all three came from the same brand? If not, the voice isn't set yet.

Marketing & Branding · Week 2Self-guided worksheet · in session
Self-guided worksheet · do it now

Draft your brand in 4 moves.

⏱ 25 min solo · 35 min group
1
Positioning
Own one word
The single association you'll defend in the customer's mind.
ExampleSafeBoda owns "safety." Yours might be "fastest," "most trusted," or "made for farmers."
2
Personality
Three traits
Pick three human traits. They decide your tone and look.
Example"Warm, rigorous, plain-spoken." Avoid vague words like "professional."
3
Logo direction
Choose a type
Which of the seven types fits — and why?
Example"A combination mark — an icon to be recognised by, the name to stay legible while we're new."
4
Brand story
One line
"We exist so that ___ can ___." Your why, in a sentence.
Example"We exist so smallholder farmers can buy seed they can actually trust."

How we'll review: each founder reads their positioning word and one-line story aloud — and the room checks whether the personality, the logo direction and the story all point at the same feeling.
