Marketing & Branding · Week 1Why the market comes first
The expensive mistake

You can't out-market a
market you don't understand.

Effective marketing isn't louder ads or a prettier logo. It's the discipline of knowing your customer better than your competitors do — their pain, their language, their wallet, and where they already spend their attention.

Get the market right and everything downstream — your brand, your channels, your pitch — gets cheaper and sharper. Get it wrong and you'll burn cash talking to people who were never going to buy.

Targeted approach

Identify and reach your ideal customer — not everyone.

Customer pain points

Are you solving a problem people will actually pay to remove?

Market opportunity

Do you have intimate knowledge of where the gap and the money are?

Marketing & Branding · Week 1From whole market to one person
Interactive · walk the steps

The segmentation journey.

Narrowing focus is not losing customers — it's earning the right to be relevant.

Step 1 · Market
The whole market
Everyone who could conceivably use a product like yours. Too broad to serve well — nobody markets to "everyone" and wins.
Step 2 · Segments
Distinct groups
Split by demographic, geographic, behavioural and psychographic traits. E.g. for a beauty brand: teens, working professionals, mature skin, diaspora.
Step 3 · Target
Your beachhead
Pick the one segment you can win first and profitably. It should be reachable, willing to pay, and underserved by competitors.
Step 4 · Persona
One real person
Compress the target into a single named human with a face, a budget and a pain. Every message you write speaks to them.
Tap each step → it expands. This is the funnel you'll run on your own business in today's exercise.

Marketing & Branding · Week 1Case study · Uganda
Case Study
🇺🇬 Qwezi Beauty · Kampala

"Have something for everyone" is a trap.

A clothing boutique's customers come in every size, age and income. The temptation is to stock for all of them. But a startup can't out-spend a market — it has to out-focus it.

Qwezi Beauty, a natural skincare brand, refused to be "beauty for everyone." It chose a precise target and built everything — product, packaging, story — around her.

The target, in one line

"Professional Black women, 21–48, searching for natural beauty products."

That single sentence tells you the product, the price point, the imagery, the channel, and the tone of voice. Focus is leverage.

Marketing & Branding · Week 1Primary vs secondary
The point of research

Read between the lines.
See what competitors miss.

Data collection

Primary: talk to customers — interviews, surveys, WhatsApp groups. Secondary: reports, publications, public data.

Customer insights

Get to the root of the problem. How they want to be reached. How much they'll actually pay.

Competitor scan

What are existing players doing about this customer issue — and where are they weak?

Market analysis

Interpret the data. What pattern do you see that the rest of the market is ignoring?

Decision-making

Define, refine, execute. Research is worthless until it changes what you do next.

The bar for founders

You don't need an agency. 15 real customer conversations beat one expensive report.

Marketing & Branding · Week 1Customer personas
From a segment to a person

Give your customer a name
and a face.

A persona is a fictional but evidence-based portrait of your ideal customer, built from your research. It keeps the whole team — and every marketing message — pointed at one real human instead of a vague crowd.

  • Personas — name, age, job, income, daily life.
  • Feedback loops — ask, listen, then actually act on it.
  • Data analytics — let behaviour, not opinion, decide.
  • Empathy — their aspirations and frustrations, in their words.

Meet "Sanyu"

34, marketing manager, Kampala → diaspora in Boston.

Wants: authentic Ugandan products without flying home for them.

Pain: shipping is unreliable; quality is a gamble; she misses home.

Pays: $100–250 a trip already — happily spends online if trust is solved.

Marketing & Branding · Week 1Unique selling proposition
Turn the gap into a sentence

The USP test.

A distinctive, compelling benefit that sets you apart — that a customer can repeat back to you.

Identify the need

How is what you're good at, good for this customer?

Define the value

Quality? Cost? Packaging? Service? Name the one you own.

Beat the field

Where do you clearly outperform or do it differently?

Say it clearly

One concise line your customer can grasp and associate with you.

Formula to fill in your exercise

For [target customer] who [need], we are the [category] that [unique benefit] — unlike [main competitor].

Marketing & Branding · Week 1From SOM to a revenue line
Make the number do work

A sized market becomes a forecast.

If 10% of 32,900 Ugandans in the US spend $1,000/yr by year 3… = $3.2M in annual sales

The average Amazon Prime account spends ~$1,400 a year — so $1,000 from a high-intent, nostalgia-driven diaspora buyer is conservative, not optimistic.

That's the move: start from a real, countable SOM, apply a defensible capture rate and spend, and you have a revenue line an investor can interrogate — which is exactly Week 4's pitch.

Marketing & Branding · Week 1Case study · positioning in a commodity market
Case Study
🇺🇬 SafeBoda · Kampala

Winning a market everyone "owned."

The market

Boda-bodas were everywhere and identical — a pure commodity. No brand, no trust, real safety fear.

The insight

Research surfaced the unserved need riders & passengers shared: safety and reliability.

The position

Helmets, training, a recognisable orange brand. They didn't sell rides — they sold trust.

Same bikes as everyone else. A different understanding of the market turned a commodity into a category-defining brand.
